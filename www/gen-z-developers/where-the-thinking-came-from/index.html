The panels

  1. 1. Where the thinking came from: Nothing in this book came from nowhere. (cover; Where the thinking came from)
  2. 2. Influences, as claims. Zara says: “Who taught you all this?” The Guide says: “An entry in this register is a falsifiable claim about my codebase.” (scene; Where the thinking came from, Why influences belong in this book)
  3. 3. Bret Victor, Inventing on Principle (2012). The Guide says: “Every step between you and the thing you are making, a compile, a deploy, a refresh, is a place whe (scene; Where the thinking came from, The opening, and learning)
  4. 4. Csikszentmihalyi: what produces the zone. Clear goals → Feedback → Challenge (spectrum; Where the thinking came from, The opening, and learning)
  5. 5. Anders Ericsson, Peak (2016). “Expertise comes from structured practice with immediate feedback at the edge of your ability, not from hours” (Where the thinking (evidence; Where the thinking came from, The opening, and learning)
  6. 6. Karl Popper. Sam says: “Prove it.” The Guide says: “a claim earns its status by being refutable” (scene; Where the thinking came from, Questioning and evidence)
  7. 7. Question everything, as a method. “the problem with dogma is not that it exists, but that you stop asking the question.” (Dinis Cruz, quoted in the register's e (evidence; Where the thinking came from, Questioning and evidence)
  8. 8. A trail of ideas the book stands on. 1945 → 1977 → 1991 → 1997 → 2005 → 2012 (spectrum; Where the thinking came from, the table)
  9. 9. Design is how it works. Priya says: “Is it simpler? Would going back feel worse?” (scene; Where the thinking came from, The villager's craft)
  10. 10. An exercise for the reader. “Know where your ideas come from; keep your own register.” (punchline; Where the thinking came from, An exercise for the reader)
