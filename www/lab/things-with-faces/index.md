# Things with faces

> An inbox, a vault, a padlock, a server and a boundary wall, each with a face in the kit's ten moods and a reaction of its own, with Vex from the lab cast pushing the wall.

*Source: <https://storyboards.sgit.ai/lab/things-with-faces/>. A standalone experiment of the Lab, status exploring, pinned to api.storyboards.sgit.ai v0.1.0. The page is drawn by code; this file is what it says.*

---

**The question.** Can a rule, a store or a boundary be a character in a storyboard rather than furniture?

## What this taught us

- A rule can be a character when it has a body that stands on the floor, a face, and one thing it does. The boundary wall became a character the moment it had an arm: the face says how it feels, the arm says what it will do, and the arm did more of the work. A wall with a face and no arm is a mural.
- Where the face sits decides whether it reads. On the tray front the inbox reads at once; the padlock's body is a face already; the server's eyes sat among its status lights and read as two more lights until they got white rings and brows; the vault's dial competes with its face, so the dial moved to a corner.
- A change reads better than a state. The inbox going from happy to worried to sad as the pile grows tells a story in a few clicks; the same worried face as a still is a worried tray. The shake and the arm, both CSS animations on the drawn group and stills under reduced motion, carried more than the brows did.
- The kit does not export its face. Brows, mouths and eyes are private to drawCharacter, so this page copies the two tables; the day the kit changes a mouth, these things drift off model. The thing to lift is a face(g, rc, mood, x, y, size, dir) export so a prop can borrow the cast's expressions.
